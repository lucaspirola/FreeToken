## Idioms adapted

**2. "call it a day" → "dar por encerrada a jornada"**
This means to stop working because you've done enough for the day. Portuguese lacks a direct equivalent, so I rendered it with "dar por encerrada a jornada" (to consider the workday closed). A simpler alternative would be "parar por hoje."

**3. "out of the woods" → "fora do buraco"**
This means being past the most dangerous or difficult part of a situation. Portuguese doesn't use "woods" (*floresta*) idiomatically here, so I substituted "fora do buraco" (out of the hole), which conveys emerging from a difficult spot. A clearer but less idiomatic option would be "fora de perigo" (out of danger).

**4. "on the right track" → "no trilho certo"**
This means heading in the correct direction. Fortunately, Portuguese shares the transportation imagery: "estar no trilho" (to be on the rail/track) is a genuine Brazilian Portuguese expression meaning things are progressing well. "No caminho certo" (on the right path) would also work.

**5. "count our chickens before they hatch" → "contar com as galinhas antes que nasçam"**
This means expecting a result before it actually happens. Portuguese has a nearly identical idiom, so only minor adaptation was needed. Note that some speakers say "contar com os **ovos** antes que nasçam" (counting the *eggs* instead of the chickens) — both versions are in common use.
